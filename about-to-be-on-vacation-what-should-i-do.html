I’m about to be on vacation. What should I do?

Before vacation, handle the few things that can actually ruin the trip, then stop planning. Confirm travel documents, transportation, reservations, money access, and your first day. Leave the rest flexible enough to enjoy.

Do the boring checks first

Make sure your ID or passport is valid, check entry rules for international travel, confirm your hotel dates, and verify the flight details in the airline app. If you need medications, pack enough for the full trip and a little extra in case of delays.

These five minutes are more useful than spending another hour looking for restaurants on social media.

Plan the first day more than the fifth day

Know how you are getting from the airport to the hotel, where you can eat if you arrive hungry, and whether you can check in when you land. The first few hours set the tone because that is when everyone is tired and carrying luggage.

After that, you can let the trip breathe.

Make a short list, not a prison schedule

Pick three to five things you really want to experience and leave room around them. If everything has a reservation attached, a delayed lunch can turn into a stressful domino effect.

Some of the best vacation moments happen because you found something while walking, stayed longer at the beach, or changed your mind.

Set your phone up before you leave

Download offline maps, save the hotel address, check your international phone plan, and store copies of important travel information somewhere you can reach without cellular service.

You can also download the airline app, transportation apps, and any tickets you will need so you are not searching for them in a crowded airport.

Then actually go on vacation

You do not need to optimize every meal, outfit, workout, and photo. The point is to experience the place, not prove that you planned it perfectly.

Once the important logistics are covered, the best thing you can do is be present enough to notice what the trip gives you.
